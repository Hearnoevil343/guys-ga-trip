### Cheapest route to get there

- **Rent, don't buy, the satellite messenger** — $100/week split 6 ways ($16.67 each) vs. $300 to buy. Biggest single lever on the budget.
- **Borrow before buying**: tent, sleeping bag, and backpack are the three heaviest cost items ($40+$80+$40 = $160 of his $600) — if any of the 5 friends already own one, his cost drops fast.
- **Buy used**: REI Re/Gear, GearTrade, or a local gear swap for the tent/pack can beat even the Walmart/Amazon budget picks above.
- **Skip Kit 4 and the "nice" rows** (pillow, power bank, knee pad, mesh bag, camp towel) — saves about $53 with no real risk.
- **Free offline maps** via CalTopo (no subscription) instead of a paid app — full-price app was an unnecessary $40/yr add.

## C. Cold-water panning tips — October, ~50°F water

- **Hands**: insulated waterproof gloves (Kit 3) are not optional at 50°F — bare-hand panning sessions will be short and painful and numb hands drop gear. Bring a dry backup pair. Chemical hand warmers in a jacket pocket for breaks. Dip hands, work fast, dry and rewarm between pans rather than staying submerged.
- **Feet**: neoprene socks under old sneakers/water shoes, not hiking boots — boots hold water and take forever to dry. Change into fully dry socks and shoes the moment you're done at the creek, every time, even for a 10-minute break. Wet feet + 30s-°F nights is the #1 way to get miserably cold.
- **Knees**: kneeling on wet gravel wicks heat fast — use the foam knee pad or kneel on a folded closed-cell pad/tarp scrap, and change out of wet pants immediately after kneeling work.
- **Keeping sleep gear dry**: run a strict two-bag system — one dry bag/stuff sack for sleep clothes, sleeping bag, and base layer that never leaves the tent vestibule wet-handed; a second bag or mesh tote for anything that touched the creek (gloves, socks, pan, classifier). Never let wet creek gear into the tent. Line the pack with a contractor-bag pack liner (cheap, and doubles as an emergency layer) so a surprise rain shower or a dunked pack doesn't reach the sleep system. Reserve one pair of wool socks that is *only* ever worn dry, inside the sleeping bag.
