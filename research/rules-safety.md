## 2. Chattahoochee-Oconee National Forest — recreational gold panning

- **Where/how allowed:** Recreational **hand panning** (gold pan + small hand shovel/trowel) is allowed forest-wide in streambeds **without a permit**, as long as there's no significant streambed/bank disturbance. — [search-summarized guidance citing fs.usda.gov/conf general info, panfortreasure.com, appenmedia.com] (2026-09-21) — treat the non-.gov sources here as secondary; the official FS permits page did not itself spell out panning rules (see below).
- **Tools:** Pan and hand trowel/shovel = OK. **No sluice boxes, suction dredges, or motorized equipment** (highbankers, dredges) — these are described as not permitted within National Forest boundaries. **Do not dig into stream banks**, panning is limited to working streambed/gravel only. — same sources, 2026-09-21. **UNVERIFIED against an official FS regulation/order** — the official permits page (fs.usda.gov/r08/chattahoochee-oconee/permits, fetched 2026-09-21) did NOT itself list gold-panning-specific rules; it only listed special-use and forest-products permit categories and district phone numbers. **Recommend calling the Blue Ridge Ranger District (706-745-6928) before the trip to confirm current pan/sluice/highbanker rules and whether a Notice of Intent is required for hand panning** (NOI is typically only required for larger-scale/mechanized operations under 36 CFR 228, not casual hand panning).
- **Wilderness areas (Blood Mountain, Mark Trail, Raven Cliffs):** Designated Wilderness prohibits **motorized equipment and mechanized transport** generally (motor vehicles, motorized tools, bicycles, carts, etc.) — [wilderness.net Blood Mountain / Raven Cliffs pages] (2026-09-21). This would rule out any mechanized prospecting gear inside Wilderness even if it were allowed elsewhere; hand panning specifically inside Wilderness boundaries is **UNVERIFIED** — call the ranger district. Practically: plan panning stops outside mapped Wilderness boundaries to avoid ambiguity, since Blood Mountain/Raven Cliffs/Mark Trail Wilderness cover much of the high terrain right around Vogel/Neel Gap.
- **Mining claims / acquired lands:** Chattahoochee NF land was acquired under the Weeks Act (not originally public domain), so it's governed as "acquired lands" mineral law rather than the 1872 General Mining Law — on acquired lands, prospecting for federally owned locatable minerals normally requires a **prospecting permit** from the Forest Service, and claims can't simply be staked the way they can on public-domain land out West. — [ecfr.gov 36 CFR Part 228](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-36/chapter-II/part-228), search summary (2026-09-21). This is a general federal-lands rule; **UNVERIFIED whether any active claims/leases exist on specific streams you plan to visit** — worth asking the ranger district if panning a specific named creek.
- **Private inholdings / AT corridor:** Not specifically researched beyond general knowledge that the National Forest is a checkerboard with private inholdings in places (e.g., near Suches, Turner's Corner). **UNVERIFIED — no specific inholding map pulled.** For the AT corridor itself, USFS/ATC generally prohibit collecting or disturbing resources within the AT protected corridor; avoid panning directly in/along the AT corridor streams without checking first.
- **District contacts:**
  - **Blue Ridge Ranger District: 706-745-6928** (covers the Vogel/Blood Mountain/Suches area) — [fs.usda.gov/r08/chattahoochee-oconee/offices/blue-ridge-ranger-district](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r08/chattahoochee-oconee/offices/blue-ridge-ranger-district), M–F 8:00–4:30 (2026-09-21)
  - **Chattooga River Ranger District: 706-754-6221** — M–F 8:00–noon, 1:00–4:30 (2026-09-21)
  - Forest Supervisor's Office (Gainesville): 770-297-3000

**Action item: call Blue Ridge RD (706-745-6928) before the trip** to confirm (a) current recreational panning rules/tool limits, (b) any temporary closures (trout spawning closures on primary trout streams — GA DNR Wildlife line 770-535-5498 per search summary, UNVERIFIED), and (c) whether your planned "remote spot" streams sit inside Wilderness or near known inholdings.

## 3. Dispersed camping (backcountry overnight)

- **Distance from water:** Camp **at least 200 feet from any water source** (streams, lakes). — search summary of FS/boondockmaps guidance (2026-09-21), **not pulled from an official FS order** — reasonable standard LNT distance but confirm with district if camping near a specific creek.
- **Stay limit:** **14-day** camping limit forest-wide (not relevant for a 1-night backcountry stay).
- **Campfires:** Dead/downed wood only for fires; don't cut live trees; don't remove wood from the forest. Fire rules vary by location/season and can be restricted.
- **Group size:** Not found in this research pass — **UNVERIFIED**, ask the ranger district if a 6-person group camping together has any size cap or if a group-use permit is needed.
- **Checking fire restrictions/burn bans for Oct 2026:** Check the **Chattahoochee-Oconee Forest Supervisor's Orders page** — [fs.usda.gov/r08/chattahoochee-oconee/about-area/forest-supervisors-orders](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r08/chattahoochee-oconee/about-area/forest-supervisors-orders) — and/or call Blue Ridge RD (706-745-6928) within a few days of the trip; Georgia typically also posts county-level burn bans via GFC (Georgia Forestry Commission). October is historically one of GA's driest months, so check close to Oct 15 for any burn ban.
- **Bear food storage:** Use bear-resistant storage / hang food at the backcountry site (standard BearWise practice); Vogel's front-country sites have steel hang-posts, but at the dispersed/backcountry site you'll need your own hang or bear-resistant bag/canister.
- **AT bear canister rule (Jarrard Gap–Neel Gap):** A **hard-sided bear canister is mandatory** for backpackers within a quarter-mile of the AT between Jarrard Gap (AT mile 26.1) and Neel Gap (AT mile 31.7) in the Blood Mountain Wilderness — but **only from March 1 to June 1 each year**. Your trip (Oct 15–21) is **outside that mandatory window**, so the canister requirement does not legally apply — but ATC still recommends canister/bear-resistant storage year-round given rising bear activity. — [appalachiantrail.org bear canister page](https://appalachiantrail.org/experience/hike-the-trail/essential-skills/safety/bears-food-storage/bear-canister-lending-programs/), [whiteblaze.net forum discussion] (2026-09-21)
