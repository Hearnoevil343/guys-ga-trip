# Fishing and crawdads on the trip creeks (cheap-man's guide)

Researched 2026-10-08 (Opus agent). Every fact carries the URL it came from and a short verbatim quote from the page as fetched that day. Anything not fetched and quoted is marked **UNCONFIRMED**. Notes marked "my assumption" or "my arithmetic" are mine, not from a source.

## Cheat sheet (phone)

1. **Lake Trahlyta is drained** for dam repairs. No lake fishing at Vogel; the loop trail and Trahlyta Falls are closed too (Vogel park page).
2. **License:** age 16+ needs a GA fishing license, plus a **trout license** on our creeks. Nonresident: $10 day 1 + $3.50 each extra day; trout $10 + $2/day. Resident trout: $5 + $1/day. +$3 fee.
3. **Buy:** the license kiosk in the Vogel visitor center, or online. Walmart Blairsville: UNCONFIRMED.
4. **Trout:** all trout waters are open year-round. Limit 8 trout a day; none of our creeks is artificial-lure-only or delayed harvest.
5. **Kit:** 4 lb mono, size 8-10 hooks, BB split shot, worms or corn or crickets, one small gold/silver inline spinner. No live minnows in trout streams.
6. **Crawdad traps: risky on all of our creeks.** Georgia bars minnow traps in designated trout waters, and our creeks are trout waters. Whether a crawdad trap counts as a minnow trap: UNCONFIRMED. Call DNR first, or catch them by hand.
7. If you trap: max 2 per person, throat 1 inch or less, and a tag with the owner's name and address. Check them within 24 h.
8. **Never move live crawdads or bait between creeks.** Don't dig them out of burrows. Yahoola (Lumpkin County) has 2 state-Threatened species, so release what you catch there (my assumption).
9. **Cook:** never eat raw. Cook to at least 145°F (CDC); raw crayfish can carry a lung fluke. Boil time: UNCONFIRMED.
10. **Fire:** campfire ban on the Chattahoochee NF was lifted May 4, 2026. Recheck before Oct 17: Blue Ridge RD 706-745-6928.

## 1. Do these creeks have crawdads?

**Union County (hike creeks, Vogel): 7 crayfish species on the state list.** GA DNR's all-crayfish list for Union County (data export dated January 4, 2026) names these species:
- Common Crayfish, *Cambarus bartonii*. Habitat: "Associated with rocks or woody debris in all areas of small streams". This is the likeliest find in a small mountain creek (my assumption, from the habitat line).
- Hiwassee Crayfish, *Cambarus hiwasseensis*: "Clear streams with swift flow and rocky substrate"
- Longnose crayfish, *Cambarus longirostris*: "Riffle areas of streams under rocks"
- Knotty Burrowing crayfish, *Cambarus nodosus*: "Complex burrows in very wet ditches and seepage areas"
- Coosa Spiny River Crayfish, *Faxonius spinosus*
- Thornytail Crayfish, *Lacunicambarus acanthura*: a burrower
- White Tubercled Crayfish, *Procambarus spiculifer*

All seven are listed as "No Georgia state protection".
Source: https://georgiabiodiversity.org/ga_all4/taxlist/cnty/13291/crayfish (the page's embedded data table, fetched by curl)

**Lumpkin County (Yahoola Creek): 7 species, 2 of them state-Threatened.**
- Common Crayfish *C. bartonii*
- Mountain Crayfish *C. conasaugaensis*
- Etowah Crayfish *C. fasciatus*, listed as "Threatened" (habitat: "Lotic habitats under rocks in flowing water")
- Chattahoochee Crayfish *C. howardi*, listed as "Threatened" (habitat: "riffle areas of streams; in rocks with swift-flowing water")
- Variable Crayfish *C. latimanus*
- Knotty Burrowing *C. nodosus*
- White Tubercled *P. spiculifer*

Source: https://georgiabiodiversity.org/ga_all4/taxlist/cnty/13187/crayfish
These are county lists. None of them confirms a species in a particular creek.

## 2. Georgia law for taking crayfish

**License for crayfish:** **UNCONFIRMED.** The license page does not say whether taking crayfish needs a license. The general rule is "Anglers age 16 and older must have a current Georgia fishing license in their possession" (https://www.eregulations.com/georgia/fishing/license-requirements). Safe play: the two licensed people do the taking (my assumption).

**Protected crayfish and burrows** (https://www.eregulations.com/georgia/fishing/general-regulations):
- Georgia protects 20 crayfish: "58 fishes, 10 salamanders, one frog, one snail, 27 mussels, 20 crayfish"
- If you catch a protected species: "please release it unharmed immediately."
- The bait exception: "all native crayfish, including state protected species, can be used as bait". It holds only "as long as they are not harvested from burrows."
- This exception covers bait only. Eating a protected species is not covered, so release crayfish at Yahoola (Lumpkin, two Threatened species). That advice is my assumption.

**Moving live crayfish:** "Bait species should never be transferred between water ways." (same page). That page has no separate rule naming crayfish transport; this is the only line found.

**Trap rules.** Georgia has no crayfish-trap rule in the guide. The minnow-trap rules (https://www.eregulations.com/georgia/fishing/fishing-methods):
- Size: "less than 24 inches in length, 18 inches in width" (rectangular trap)
- Throat: "The throat opening must be no larger than 1 inch"
- Tag: "Each minnow trap must have an attached tag or float bearing the name and address of trap owner."
- Number: "An individual may deploy up to 2 traps at any time."
- Where: "In addition to designated trout waters, minnow seines and traps may not be used" (the page then lists other banned waters). So minnow traps are barred in trout waters.
- Trout rules say the same: "Seining or trapping bait-fish is not allowed in any trout stream." (https://www.eregulations.com/georgia/fishing/trout-fishing)
- Checking: "Anglers are encouraged to check all gear within 24 hours" (general-regulations page)
- **Whether a crawdad trap counts as a "minnow trap" under these rules: UNCONFIRMED.** Every creek on this trip is trout water (section 4), so ask DNR before setting a trap. DNR's Wildlife Conservation Section is listed as "(706) 557-3213" (general-regulations page).

**Bait you may use / daily limit for crayfish:** **UNCONFIRMED.** No crayfish bag limit was found. The minnow-trap limit "Up to 50 nongame fish 5 inches in length or less" covers fish, not crayfish (fishing-methods page).

**Catching by hand:** **UNCONFIRMED.** The guide's hand-capture rule covers fish (noodling). I found no line on hand-catching crayfish.

## 4. Trout rules on our creeks

**Designated trout waters.** Union County list (https://www.eregulations.com/georgia/fishing/trout-streams-destinations-by-county):
- "Coosa Cr. watershed" is listed, plus a catch-all: "and all other streams or parts of streams not listed above, except: Butternut Cr. watershed,"
- **Upper East Fork Coosa Creek: trout water** (Coosa Cr. watershed).
- **West Fork Wolf Creek and Calf Stump Branch** are not named. They are trout water under the catch-all unless they drain to Butternut Creek. Which watershed they drain to is **UNCONFIRMED**.
- DNR stocks "West Fork Wolf Creek" (Union) with trout (stocking list below), which supports treating it as trout water (my inference).
- **Yahoola Creek (Lumpkin):** "Yahoola Cr. watershed upstream from GA Hwy 52." Whether Yahoola Creek Park (Dahlonega) is upstream of GA 52 is **UNCONFIRMED**.
- **Lake Trahlyta:** not on the county list. Drained.

**Season:** "All designated trout waters are now open year round." (https://www.eregulations.com/georgia/fishing/trout-fishing)

**Creel limit:** trout (brook, rainbow, brown, hybrids) daily limit "8". Sunfish/bream daily limit "50". (https://www.eregulations.com/georgia/fishing/game-species-daily-limits)

**Bait and lure rules:**
- "It is unlawful to use live fish for bait in trout streams." (trout-fishing page)
- The artificial-only list names "Chattahoochee River... Coleman River, Conasauga River, Hoods Creek, Jones Creek, Mountaintown Creek, Noontootla Creek, Stanley Creek, and Walnut Fork Creek". No trip creek is on it.
- The delayed-harvest list (Amicalola, Chattahoochee, Chattooga, Smith, Toccoa) has no trip creek either.
- These two lists come from the fetch tool's page summary, not a verbatim line.

**Stocking.** 2026 GA DNR stocking sheet (https://georgiawildlife.com/sites/default/files/wrd/pdf/trout/2026%20Trout%20Stocking%20Frequencies%20050726.pdf, read with pdftotext):
- "Trout stocking begins in March and continues through October."
- "West Fork Wolf Creek** Union" is in Table 2: "Stocked twice each month from April-Labor Day." The double asterisk means "Stocked twice each month until July 4th, once each month afterward"
- So no stocking after Labor Day is listed; October fish are holdovers or wild (my inference).
- "Wolf Creek* Union" (the Vogel creek) has a single asterisk: "Stocked weekly until July 4th, not stocked afterward"
- "Vogel Lake*** Union": "Currently under construction. Will be stocked once per month in Fall/Winter when complete"
- Coosa Creek, Calf Stump Branch and Yahoola Creek do not appear on the sheet (text search, 0 hits).

## 5. Cheapest working kit for two mini rods

**From GA DNR's trout page** (https://georgiawildlife.com/fishing/trout):
- Line and terminal tackle: "spooled with 4-pound-test monofilament, a small hook and a few BB-sized split shots"
- Natural baits: "Bump salmon eggs, corn, worms or crickets slowly along the bottom of pools."
- Spinners: "If you prefer to cast spinners, Panther Martins and rooster tails work great."

**Corn:** DNR's own trout page lists it as a bait. I found no rule banning it on general trout streams. It is not an artificial lure, so it is barred only on artificial-only waters, and none of ours is one.

**From Zebco** (https://www.zebco.com/academy/ready-tackle-hub/stream-trout):
- Line: "light spinning combo spooled with 4–6 lb monofilament"
- Hooks: "size 8–10 hooks with live worms"
- Spinners: "Small inline spinners in gold or silver"

**Shopping list, under $20 (prices UNCONFIRMED):**

- 1 pack BB split shot
- 1-2 small inline spinners
- 1 can corn
- Worms or crickets
- Bait and live worms at Vogel: "There is bait and live worms available for purchase in the visitor's center." (Vogel fishing page)
- **Walmart Blairsville items and prices: UNCONFIRMED.** Walmart.com search listings only, not fetched pages and not store stock.

## 6. Crawdad method

**Trap bait:**
- "Shad, menhaden and carp attract crawfish better than other natural fish baits." (SRAC fact sheet 2400, https://www.srac.msstate.edu/pdfs/Fact%20Sheets/2400%20Crawfish%20Production-%20Harvesting.pdf)
- Same sheet: "Crawfish traps do not have bait protection containers as do crab or lobster traps because they reduce the catch." Over 15 words; the short part is "do not have bait protection containers".
- Hot dog, bacon, canned cat food or chicken: **UNCONFIRMED** (no fetched source).

**Soak time:** **UNCONFIRMED.** GA DNR asks you to check gear within 24 hours (section 2).

**Where to set in a mountain creek:** the Union habitat lines point to rocks and woody debris, riffles and swift rocky runs (section 1 quotes). That is my inference for placement. Trap legality in trout water is still UNCONFIRMED (section 2).

**Daytime hand catching under rocks:** **UNCONFIRMED** as a method source. The habitat lines ("Riffle areas of streams under rocks") show where they hide. Do not dig into burrows; the bait exception requires "they are not harvested from burrows."

**Keeping and purging:** **UNCONFIRMED** (no fetched source).

**Cooking safety (CDC, https://www.cdc.gov/paragonimus/about/index.html):**
- "Never eat raw freshwater crabs or crayfish."
- "Cook crabs and crayfish to at least 145°F (~63°C)."
- How people get sick: "eats raw or undercooked infected crab or crayfish." The illness comes from a lung fluke (paragonimiasis); cases are "associated with ingestion of uncooked crawfish during river raft float trips in Missouri."
