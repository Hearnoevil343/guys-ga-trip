# Panning gloves (research only, not in the gear list)

Started 2026-09-23. Re-checking because the Captain doesn't like the current picks (PVC gauntlet gloves) in [BUY_LIST.md](../../BUY_LIST.md). Prices from Amazon/vendor listings and paddling/fishing review sites. Re-check before buying.

## Answer in one line
**Stay with dry gauntlets, upgraded:** Arctic Waterproof Gauntlet Gloves ($39.95, insulated/lined) instead of the bare PVC ones. **Switch to dexterity:** Glacier Glove Kenai neoprene gloves ($20-25) if you'd rather feel the pan and pick out gravel than stay bone-dry.

## The real trade-off
Two different tools, not a "better" and "worse":
- **Gauntlet (PVC/rubber, arm-length):** hand and forearm stay completely dry, room for a liner glove underneath. No dexterity — you can't feel nuggets or pick through gravel with them on, and they're bulky/clumsy for anything but scooping and swirling.
- **Neoprene (wetsuit-style, wrist-length):** water gets in and your skin gets wet, but the neoprene traps and warms a layer against your hand, and you keep full finger dexterity for actually working the pan, picking up flakes, or handling tools. Not any warmer than a PVC gauntlet in genuinely cold water — it just changes what you give up.

Not recommended: cheap $2 nylon garden gloves (no insulation, TreasureNet's own "warm weather only" note) and full diving gauntlet mitts (lose all dexterity, made for depth not stream work).

## Recommended combo
- **If staying dry matters more** (repeated dipping, cold October mornings, kids/inexperienced hands): Arctic Waterproof Gauntlet Gloves, $39.95. Direct upgrade over the current pick, same category, just insulated.
- **If working the pan matters more** (sorting gravel, picking flakes, handling tools without gloves on/off): Glacier Glove Kenai Original, ~$20-25. Cheaper than the gauntlet upgrade and better dexterity, but hands get wet.
- Could also do both: neoprene gloves for actual panning, dry gauntlets on hand for cold dips or cleanup.
