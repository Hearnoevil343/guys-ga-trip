# Cook system: fuel nests in the pot (research only, not in the gear list)

Started 2026-09-23. Specs from Switchback Travel and other review sites, NOT vendor pages. Re-check before buying. Jetboil Stash price/weight are from my memory (~$150, ~7 oz), not the reviews; verify.

## Update: small pot, all-in-one nest (Capitan's preference)
**Jetboil Stash** is the pick. 0.8 L pot, 7.1 oz total (confirmed by REI, OutdoorGearLab, GearJunkie), boils 0.5 L in about 2.5 min. Stove, tripod and a 100 g canister all stow inside the pot. Price ~$150 (still unverified, check REI). Trade-offs: small pot, boil/simple-cook only, no big simmer.
Alternative if you want titanium/lighter DIY: Soto WindMaster + Toaks ~750 ml Ti pot + 4 oz canister. Do NOT buy the SOTO ThermoLite set alone, it has no burner.

## Update: under $100
1. **Fire-Maple Fixed Star 1** - ~$49 (Amazon, fluctuates). Heat-exchanger pot, piezo igniter, 1 L pot. Burner, stand and a 230 g canister all fit inside. ~16-18 oz, heavier and bigger than you wanted, but the best real integrated system under $100 and ~30% faster boil.
2. **Toaks 750 ml Ti pot + small burner (~$85 total, DIY)** - pot is 3.6 oz (~$40, unverified). A 4 oz canister and a small stove fit inside. Burner: Soto Amicus (~$45, verify price) or MSR PocketRocket 2 ($50). Smallest and lightest under $100. Not integrated, so you build it.
3. **Snow Peak Ti-Mini Solo / GSI Microdualist** - nesting sets with room for stove + 110 g canister; price not checked.

Correction: the Amicus Cookset Combo link I gave earlier was an Australian store (AUD price), and that page said no igniter. Check US listing for the igniter and price before buying.

## Top picks
1. **Jetboil MiniMo** - the most "amazing" complete system that also cooks real food.
2. **Soto WindMaster + Toaks 900 ml titanium pot** - lightest and cleverest nest, if you want the DIY route.
3. **Soto Amicus Combo** - $50 and still nests.

## Notes for this trip
- Heat-exchanger pots (Jetboil/WindBurner) are best at boiling water for freeze-dried meals, coffee, oatmeal. Frying or real cooking needs a wide pot (Toaks / Amicus).
- Georgia weather rarely needs the Reactor or a pressure-regulated cold-weather stove. Wind is the main issue.
- Canisters can't fly. Buy fuel at the destination.
- Some areas ban wood stoves, so skip those.

## Fuel for PocketRocket 2 (my estimate, not from a source)
- Any threaded isobutane/propane canister fits: MSR IsoPro, Jetboil JetPower, Snow Peak GigaPower. Sizes 110 g (4 oz), 227 g (8 oz), 450 g. Not the pierce-type camping-stove cans.
- Rule of thumb: ~10-12 g per 0.5 L boil, wind adds more. 4 days x 2.5 meals = 10 boils, roughly 100-150 g, one person.
- Buy: one 227 g can (safe margin). Or one 110 g if only boiling water, plus a 2nd 110 g as backup. Two people: roughly double.
- 110 g fits inside the Toaks 750 ml pot with the stove; 227 g does not.
- Weigh a partly used can to see how much is left; canisters are sold at outdoor stores/Walmart near the destination (cannot fly).
