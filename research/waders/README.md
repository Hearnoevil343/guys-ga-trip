Captain wants to upgrade anyway: not deep, but done right. Prices from Amazon/vendor listings, re-check before buying.

## Answer in one line
**FROGG TOGGS Canyon II Stockingfoot Hip Wader, $99.99 MSRP** — breathable (not the sweaty rubber the current pick is), highest-rated breathable hip wader on the market, and hip-height is already more coverage than shin-deep panning needs. Don't go to chest waders — nothing on this trip goes deep enough to use the extra height.

**Live price check (2026-09-23):** froggtoggs.com direct $115, waders.com $115, Tractor Supply $99.99, **Rogers Sporting Goods $79.99** (marked down from $99.99 — current best price found). ## Why hip, not chest
[rules-safety.md](../rules-safety.md) and the panning-glove research both land on the same fact: Chattahoochee NF rules are hand-pan + trowel only, no bank digging, and the community consensus is nobody stands in more than shin-deep water doing this. Chest waders solve a problem this trip doesn't have. The one real upgrade worth paying for is breathable fabric — rubber/PVC hip waders (the current pick) trap sweat and heat even in cool October water; a breathable stockingfoot wader doesn't.

## Recommendation
Frogg Toggs Canyon II Hip Wader (~$85-100) is the correctly-sized "done right": breathable upgrade over the current pick, hip height already exceeds what shin-deep panning needs, no wasted capability. The Orvis Ultralight Convertible is the better wader in an absolute sense and doubles as real fishing gear later, but it's $350 solving a depth problem you don't have on this trip — only go there if you want the do-everything wader for future trips, not for this one.

**Verdict: stay with the Canyon II.** Kylebooker is the only genuine same-category competitor found, and it doesn't beat the Canyon II — it's a comparable price for a thinner bootie and lighter fabric from a less-established brand. The one edge Kylebooker has (built-in gravel guard) isn't worth trading 1mm of bootie thickness and brand track record for. No other brand makes a breathable hip wader in this price band; the rest of the market is chest waders.

## Considered and rejected
**FROGG TOGGS Rana Emergency Compact Packable Chest Wader, ~$25.** Same brand as the recommended Canyon II, but a different product line entirely — thin PVC, not breathable, designed to roll up and live in a glovebox/tackle box for emergencies, not for repeated use. Reviews report the bootie ripping by the second wear on sticks/gravel. Wrong tool for a multi-session creek trip on rocky ground — the Canyon II is Frogg Toggs' actual durable line and worth the extra cost over this one.
