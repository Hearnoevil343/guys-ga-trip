## 1. How to pan, and what is in the pan

### Sources

**A. ICMJ Prospecting and Mining Journal, "Gold Panning Instructions" (courtesy of Keene Engineering)** [direct]
https://www.icmj.com/resources/helpful-links/beginners-corner/gold-panning-instructions-3/
Trade journal and equipment maker, not a government source.

- "Fill pan 3/4 full of gravel, then submerge it deep enough so it is just under the surface of the water. Give the pan several vigorous shakes back and forth and from side to side, but not too vigorous as to wash material out of the pan."
- "If roots and moss surface, work them over your pan with your fingers to dissolve any lumps. Pick out the larger rocks after making sure that they are washed clean."
- "Hold the pan just under the water and tilt it slightly away from you. Begin to swirl the water from side to side, with a slight forward tossing motion."
- "Leveling the pan from time to time and shaking it back and forth will cause the light material to come to the surface and the gold to settle to the bottom."
- "Repeat Process 1 and 2 of Step B until there is only about two cups of heavier material left in your pan. This material is usually called "black sand" or "concentrate.""
- "raise the pan completely out of the water, leaving about an inch of water in it. Tilt the pan slightly towards you and swirl the water slowly in a circular motion to check the pan for nuggets and pieces that can easily be picked out by hand."
- "If you have a plastic pan, the use of a magnet can be employed to quickly aid in the separation of gold from the black, magnetic sand concentrate."
- Site choice: "Select a spot where the water is a minimum of six inches deep and flowing just fast enough to keep the muddy water from impairing your vision of your pan".

**B. USGS FAQ, "What is Fool's Gold?"** [relayed, fetched twice with the same wording]
https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-fools-gold

- "Fool's Gold can be one of three minerals. The most common mineral mistaken for gold is pyrite. Chalcopyrite may also appear gold-like, and weathered mica can mimic gold as well."
- These minerals "will flake, powder, or crumble when poked with a metal point, whereas gold will gouge or indent like soft lead."
- "Actual gold will leave a golden yellow streak when scraped on a piece of unglazed porcelain. Pyrite and chalcopyrite will leave a dark green to black streak and the common micas will leave a white streak."

**C. USGS, "Prospecting for Gold in the United States"** [relayed]
https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/prospect2/prospectgip.html

- "The process of washing material in a pan, referred to as 'panning,' is the simplest and most commonly used and least expensive method for a prospector to separate gold from the silt, sand, and gravel of the stream deposits."

**D. 911Metallurgist, "Gold Prospecting" (2015)** [relayed; commercial site, weak source]
https://www.911metallurgist.com/blog/gold-prospecting/

- "The specific gravity of gold is approximately 19, while that of gravel is only 2.7."
- Gold collects at "the inside of a bend, the bottom of the waterfall, trailing edge of sand or a gravel bar, or behind a large boulder" and "Underneath the moss on top of the bedrock."

## 2. Is panning legal at the three creeks?

### National Forest creeks (West Fork Wolf Creek, East Fork Coosa Creek)

No source names either creek. What applies is the general National Forest rule.

**36 CFR 228.4(a)(1)(ii)** [relayed, via Cornell LII] https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/36/228.4
A notice of intent is not required for: "Prospecting and sampling which will not cause significant surface resource disturbance and will not involve removal of more than a reasonable amount of mineral deposit for analysis and study which generally might include searching for and occasionally removing small mineral samples or specimens, gold panning, metal detecting, non-motorized hand sluicing, using battery operated dry washers, and collecting of mineral specimens using hand tools;"

**USDA Forest Service, "Rockhounding Guide", FS-1091, June 2017** [direct, PDF text]
https://www.fs.usda.gov/media/229394

- "Most national forest lands are open for rock and mineral collecting, metal detecting, gold panning, and prospecting."
- Plan of Operations generally not required: "Low-impact activity with little surface disturbance (e.g., gold panning, hand collection of gems, minerals, and rocks. Metal Detecting."
- Plan of Operations may be required: "Use of small sluice box or rocker box with a shovel."
- "Depending on your location and the land ownership, additional permits may be required from State and /or local agencies."

**Chattahoochee-Oconee NF, "Other" recreation page (rockhounding)** [relayed]
https://fs.usda.gov/r08/chattahoochee-oconee/recreation/opportunities/other
The page covers rockhounding and does not mention gold panning.

- "Special permission, permits, or fees are not required to take a handful of rock, mineral, or petrified wood specimens from the surface of National Forest lands for personal use."
- "No mechanical equipment may be used and any collection must not conflict with existing mineral permits, leases, claims, or sales."
- "Generally, a reasonable amount is up to 10 pounds."

**Appen Media column, Steve Hudson, 2016, updated 2021** [relayed; secondary]
https://www.appenmedia.com/opinion/columnists/where-can-i-go-to-prospect-for-gold/article_b3474556-585c-5e24-bd04-0b55dc94fa8a.html
This is the only source found for the forest-specific "pan and trowel, stream bed only, no sluice or dredge" wording. It is not on the current Forest Service page.

- "Panning is limited to recreational panning within streambeds only"
- tools: "a small hand shove or trowel and gold pan" (typo in the original)

**Group size: 36 CFR 251.51** [relayed, via Cornell LII] https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/36/251.51
Group use is "an activity conducted on National Forest System lands that involves a group of 75 or more people, either as participants or spectators." Five people is far below the permit threshold.

**Findings**

- Permit: none needed for hand panning on National Forest land (federal rule and FS-1091).
- Tools: pan and hand tools. The hub's "no sluice" rule is stricter than the federal text, which lists non-motorized hand sluicing. The stricter forest wording has only the 2021 newspaper column behind it. Keep the hub rule; it is the safe reading.
- Group of 5: no group permit (threshold is 75).
- Wilderness ban: confirmed by Jordan 2026-09-21; no Forest Service page found stating it.
- Coosa Bald National Scenic Area: no rule found either way. unconfirmed. Stays on the ranger call.
- Creek-specific closures or orders: none found, none ruled out. Stays on the ranger call.

### Yahoola Creek Park (Lumpkin County)

**Lumpkin County Code of Ordinances, Chapter 34, Parks and Recreation** [direct]
https://library.municode.com/ga/lumpkin_county/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=PTIICOOR_CH34PARE
Codified through Resolution 2026-37 (June 16, 2026). The chapter has articles for the Community House, Canoe Launch Park and Blackburn Park only. Nothing on Yahoola Creek Park, digging, minerals or panning.

**"Yahoola Creek Park and Community Center Rules"** [direct, PDF text; file dated 2014-10-28]
https://www.lumpkincounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/235/Yahoola-Creek-Park-and-Community-Center-Rules-PDF
Ten rules (alcohol, profanity, glass, cleats, property damage, child supervision, smoking, speed and parking, ejection, complaints). None mentions panning, digging or the creek. Closest: "Anyone found destroying park property will be fined for restitution. Violators will be prosecuted." Also: "No glass containers allowed."

**Lumpkin County Parks and Recreation page** [direct] https://www.lumpkincounty.gov/202/Rules
"Park Hours Monday - Sunday: 7:30 AM - 9:00 PM". Phone 706-864-3622.

**Findings**

- No written county rule allows or forbids panning. The hub's "reported as tolerated" has no source: unconfirmed.
- The address 1166 Captain McDonald Rd appeared only in a search summary of lumpkincounty.gov, not in a fetched page: unconfirmed.
- Glass is banned in the park: use plastic sample vials there.
- The county call (CALLS.md item 5) is still required.

### Vogel and other state parks

**Georgia State Parks, Park Rules** [relayed] https://gastateparks.org/ParkRules

- "All wildlife, plant life, driftwood, artifacts and any other natural or man-made features are protected and may not be disturbed or removed."
- "Possession of metal detecting equipment is prohibited."

The Appen column [relayed] adds: "Gold panning, prospecting and rock collecting are not permitted within Georgia's state parks."

## 3. Brasstown Bald parking lot at night

**Forest Service, Brasstown Bald Visitor Information Center page, "Last updated August 5, 2026"** [direct]
https://www.fs.usda.gov/r08/chattahoochee-oconee/recreation/brasstown-bald-visitor-information-center

- "7 days a week 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM"
- "Shuttle Schedule: Last departure: 4:30 PM Last return: 5:00 PM"
- "Regular Hours: $10/adult (Ages 16+)   After Hours: $6/adult (Ages 16+)   Free for children 15 and younger."
- "Restroom information is not available for this site." "Potable water is not available at this site."
- "The Brasstown Bald Summit Trail, which connects the parking area to the Visitor Center and Viewing Platform, is a 0.6 mile paved foot trail. It is very steep." [relayed]
- Interagency Senior and Access passes are accepted [relayed].

**Explore Georgia listing** [relayed]
https://exploregeorgia.org/hiawassee/outdoors-nature/hiking/brasstown-bald-visitors-center-recreation-area

- "Gates to Brasstown Bald will be closed in inclement weather. Call before your visit to confirm that facilities are open and to confirm directions."
- The fetched page showed no after-hours or 24/7 statement. A search summary attributed "accessible 24/7" and "a single pit toilet ... after hours" to this listing, but the fetch did not reproduce either: unconfirmed.

**Blog, paigemindsthegap.com, 2023, updated 2024** [relayed; not official]
https://paigemindsthegap.com/brasstown-bald-at-sunset/

- "The observation platform is always accessible as long as the road up the mountain is open."

**Findings**

- The Forest Service does publish an after-hours fee ($6 per adult 16+), so being there after 5 PM is an expected use. This is new: the Forest Service, not only Explore Georgia, now supports after-hours access.
- No source states how late the gate or lot stays open, or that it is open all night. The 7:50-10:15 PM window is still unconfirmed.
- The gate closes in bad weather.
- Fee for 5 adults after hours: $30. How it is paid after hours (envelope, cash) is unconfirmed.
- No shuttle after 5 PM: the summit is a steep 0.6 mile paved walk each way.
- Visitor center phone from a search summary only: 706-896-4137 (unconfirmed).
- The ranger call stays, narrowed to one question: is the gate on Spur 180 open until about 10:30 PM in mid-October?
