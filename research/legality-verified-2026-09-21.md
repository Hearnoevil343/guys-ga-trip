## Q1 — Panning / rockhounding / mineral collecting

### Gold panning — tools, prohibitions, permits
**CONFIRMED.** Source: Chattahoochee-Oconee NF official FAQ page, fetched via curl (raw HTML parsed), page footer dated "Last updated February 25, 2026."
URL: https://www.fs.usda.gov/r08/chattahoochee-oconee/about-area/faqs

> "Is gold panning, rockhounding, and fossil collecting allowed on the National Forest? Yes, however there are strict rules and limitations which you must follow. Recreational panning for gold in most stream beds is allowed. Special permission, permits, or fees are not required as long as significant stream disturbance does not occur and when only a small hand shovel or trowel and a pan are used. In-stream sluices and suction dredges are NOT allowed."

### Rockhounding — quantity, tools
**CONFIRMED.** Same page/URL as above.

> "Recreational rockhounding may take place at areas where minerals are loose and free on the surface. Special permission, permits, or fees are not required to take a handful of rock, mineral, or petrified wood specimens from the surface of National Forest lands for personal use. You can collect a specimen if you can see all or part of it exposed on the surface of the ground. You can remove up to 6 inches of soil immediately around the specimen you are collecting. Do not dig so much as to cause significant surface disturbance that leads to damage of natural resources. You may collect reasonable amounts of specimens. Generally, a reasonable amount is up to 10 pounds. You can only collect specimens for personal use and non-commercial gain."
> "No mechanical equipment may be used."

### Fossil collecting — Wilderness exclusion
**CONFIRMED**, but note this Wilderness exclusion is stated for **fossils**, not explicitly for gold panning/rockhounding. Same page/URL.

> "Fossil collecting for commercial purposes is not allowed. However, casual collection of common and abundant invertebrate and plant fossils may be allowed for personal use, provided: you are not in a designated Wilderness area; you are collecting for non-commercial purposes...; you do not create significant ground disturbance, and; you are not using any mechanized equipment"

### Wilderness — panning/mineral collection specifically
**UNCONFIRMED as a blanket ban.** The Chattahoochee-Oconee FAQ (above) does not state that gold panning or rockhounding (as opposed to fossil collecting) is prohibited in Wilderness. The national USDA Forest Service Rockhounding Guide (referenced by the same FAQ page) is deliberately non-committal:

Source: USDA Forest Service Rockhounding Guide (FS-1091, June 2017), fetched as PDF and read directly.
URL: https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/rockhounding-guide-fseprd768373.pdf

> "In national monuments, wilderness areas, and other special or protected areas, collecting may or may not be allowed. Check with the local ranger district for information."

Do not treat any stronger "Wilderness bans all mineral collecting" claim as confirmed — that language does not appear in either official document. (A web-search AI summary asserted a flat Wilderness Act ban; that claim was NOT found in the primary sources and should be disregarded.)

### Georgia State Parks — prospecting/collecting rule
**CONFIRMED** (general "Collecting & Defacing" park rule; does not use the word "prospecting" specifically, but plainly covers it). Fetched via curl, raw HTML parsed.
URL: https://gastateparks.org/ParkRules

> "All wildlife, plant life, driftwood, artifacts and any other natural or man-made features are protected and may not be disturbed or removed. Please do not leave painted rocks or other structures in parks. Painting, carving or vandalizing rocks, structures or other natural resources is not allowed... Possession of metal detecting equipment is prohibited."

Note: this rule applies within Georgia State Park boundaries (e.g., Vogel State Park itself). It does not apply once the Coosa Backcountry Trail crosses onto National Forest land outside the park boundary — there, the USFS panning/rockhounding rules above apply instead.

## Q4 — Coosa Bald National Scenic Area

Source: 16 U.S.C. § 460ggg-1 (Office of the Law Revision Counsel, U.S. House), "Text contains those laws in effect on September 20, 2026." Fetched directly, full section read.
URL: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title16-section460ggg-1&num=0&edition=prelim

> "§460ggg–1. National scenic area — (a) Designation and purposes — For the purposes of protecting and enhancing the natural beauty, special ecological features, watershed integrity, mature-forest habitat, scenic recreation opportunities and other distinctive values of certain lands in Georgia, the lands in the Chattahoochee National Forest, Georgia, which comprise approximately 7,100 acres, as generally depicted on a map entitled "Coosa Bald Scenic Area-Proposed", dated July 1991, are designated as a national scenic area and shall be known as the Coosa Bald National Scenic Area..."
> "(b)(1) Subject to valid existing rights, the Secretary shall administer the scenic area in accordance with the laws, rules, and regulations applicable to the National Forest System..."
> "(b)(5) The scenic area is hereby withdrawn from the operation of all laws pertaining to mineral leasing."
> "(b)(7) The Secretary shall protect, enhance, and promote the public's opportunities for primitive and semiprimitive experiences in the scenic area."
> Source: "(Pub. L. 102–217, §3, Dec. 11, 1991, 105 Stat. 1667.)"

**CONFIRMED: this is a withdrawal from mineral LEASING law only** (i.e., commercial mining-claim/leasing law under Title 30), administered "subject to valid existing rights." **The statute does not say anything about recreational gold panning or camping being restricted** — as anticipated, a mineral-leasing withdrawal is not the same as a ban on recreational panning, and the text does not mention panning, rockhounding, or camping at all. No general camping restriction is stated in this section. (Georgia Wilderness Act of 1991 is Pub. L. 102-217; this section, §3, is the Coosa Bald designation; §4/16 U.S.C. §460ggg-2, also fetched, is the adjacent Ed Jenkins (formerly "Springer Mountain") National Recreation Area, which has an identical mineral-leasing-withdrawal clause and no panning/camping restriction either.)

- No Forest Order was found at all for Coosa Bald/Blood Mountain Wilderness/Duncan Ridge/Coosa Backcountry Trail — reported as "none found," not assumed either way.
- The gadnr.org "Sat. Oct. 18" firearms-season headline is internally inconsistent with the 2026 calendar (Oct 18, 2026 is a Sunday) and could not be fetched to verify; downgraded to unconfirmed rather than repeated as fact.
