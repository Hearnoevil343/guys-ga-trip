# GA Backcountry Gold-Panning Overnight — Wide Net (Round 2)

Prepared 2026-09-21. Base camp Vogel State Park, Neel Gap, GA (34.7638, -83.9263).
This file covers candidates NOT already researched in `overnight-route.md`
(skips Frogtown/DeSoto Falls, Chestatee headwaters, Boggs Creek, Dockery Lake,
Etowah/Hightower Gap, Nimblewill/Jones Creek, Tesnatee Creek, Dicks Creek —
see that file for those). Drive times below are **estimated from road geometry
and known distances, not routed** — mark as such and verify with a mapping

invented.

no banks/no motorized gear, banned in designated Wilderness, Scenic River
corridors, and state parks. Firearms deer season opens Oct 17, 2026 — wear
blaze orange region-wide during the trip window.

## Candidate A (TOP PICK): Three Forks / Noontootla Creek, Benton MacKaye & Appalachian Trail junction, Fannin/Union line

**Trailhead:** Three Forks, at the AT/Benton MacKaye Trail junction on FS Road 58, Noontootla Creek. Coordinates from a trailhead-access writeup: **N34°39.815' W084°11.053' = 34.6636, -84.1842** (converted from minutes). [N2Backpacking BMT access directions](https://n2backpacking.com/hikes/georgia/benton-mackaye-trail-big-stamp-gap-to-three-forks/)
**Access:** From Blue Ridge/Aska Rd area: Aska Road 13.5 mi to its end, right on Newport Rd 4.5 mi, left on Doublehead Gap Rd, cross a concrete bridge, continue gravel 0.3 mi to FS 58, turn right (east) 5.3 mi to Three Forks. Gravel FS road, high-clearance recommended for the last stretch; trucks fine. Park along the road at Three Forks — no formal lot. [N2Backpacking](https://n2backpacking.com/hikes/georgia/benton-mackaye-trail-big-stamp-gap-to-three-forks/)

**Route:** Three Forks is the confluence of Stover, Chester, and Long Creeks, which join to form Noontootla Creek. [Backpacker.com – BMT Springer Mtn to Three Forks](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/benton-mackaye-trail-springer-mountain-to-three-forks-section-1/) Hike a short distance up the AT or BMT from the Three Forks car-camp cluster to get away from the road-accessible crowd, or walk downstream along Noontootla Creek itself (FS 58 parallels part of it) to a quieter gravel bar. One-way distance to a private-feeling site: **~1–2 mi, UNVERIFIED exact mileage** — this is a short, easy overnight well within the group's 5 mi/day pace; good for the 4 newer hikers.

**Campsite(s):** Established backcountry campsites cluster at Three Forks itself (heavily used — car-camping and thru-hiker traffic); for more solitude, camp along Noontootla Creek upstream/downstream of the confluence. No separate marked backcountry site coordinates found beyond the Three Forks junction point above; **UNVERIFIED** exact tent-pad locations.

**Water:** Three Forks/Noontootla Creek — reliable perennial water, treat before drinking.

**Gold evidence:** The U.S. Forest Service's first Georgia land purchase (1911) was a 31,000-acre tract bought from the Gennett brothers specifically named the "**Rock Creek and Noontootly Creek area** of Fannin, Gilmer, Lumpkin and Union counties" — placing Noontootla Creek inside a named historic mining/timber tract. [Blue Ridge WMA history summary](https://gon.com/hunting/the-story-of-blue-ridge-the-first-wma) thediggings.com's Fannin County gold-mine index lists placer workings including a "**Hackney Placer**" and mining along "**Noontootly Creek**" specifically. [The Diggings – Fannin County gold mines](https://thediggings.com/usa/georgia/fannin-ga111/mines?commodity=gold) A separate dispersed-camping area named "Noontootla Dispersed Area" exists just downstream on the same creek, confirming public NF access along its length. [Campflare – Noontootla Dispersed Area](https://campflare.com/campground/noontootla-dispersed-area-762)

**Land status:** Chattahoochee NF, Blue Ridge Ranger District, standard NF land — **not** Wilderness (Blood Mountain/Mark Trail Wilderness boundaries are well north/east of this drainage). Recreational hand panning should be legal on Stover/Chester/Long/Noontootla Creek reaches here under standard NF rules; **confirm with Blue Ridge RD before the trip** since no district-specific written confirmation for this exact reach was found.

**Hazards:** Creek crossings (Long Creek Falls nearby — do not pan near the falls, slick rock); shared corridor with AT thru-hikers (Oct is a moderate-traffic month, mostly southbound finishers); standard black-bear country, hang food.

**Crowding:** Three Forks itself is a known, fairly popular car-camping/thru-hiker waypoint — expect other campers at the junction. Moving even a half-mile up Long Creek or downstream on Noontootla reduces this substantially.

**Vibe/photos:** Noontootla Creek — rhododendron-lined mountain stream, moderate gradient, gravel/cobble bars typical of Georgia placer creeks. [Wikimedia Commons – Noontootla Creek photos](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Noontootla_Creek,_Fannin_County_Georgia_-_panoramio.jpg) (34.6737, -84.1972, geosearch match)

**Drive time from Vogel:** Estimated 40–50 min via US 19/129 N to Suches (GA 60) then Aska Rd corridor — **not routed, verify with a map before departure.**

## Candidate B: Rock Creek dispersed area (Fannin Co.) — hike upstream from road-end

**Trailhead/access:** Rock Creek Dispersed Area, Chattahoochee NF, Fannin Co. Coordinates: **34.7214, -84.1331** (Campflare listing). [Campflare – Rock Creek Dispersed Area](https://campflare.com/campground/rock-creek-dispersed-area-757) Access: Skeenah Gap Rd south 7.8 mi, left on Hwy 60 S 3.4 mi, right on Rock Creek Rd (gravel FS road, follows the creek past the GA DNR Rock Creek trout hatchery). [Campflare](https://campflare.com/campground/rock-creek-dispersed-area-757)

**Route:** This is fundamentally a **drive-to dispersed-camping road**, not a hike-in trailhead — to make it a true backcountry overnight, drive trucks to the end of the maintained gravel (where sites thin out) and hike upstream along the Rock Creek valley on old roadbed/streamside ground to a private site away from the campground cluster and the hatchery. One-way hiking distance: **UNVERIFIED, likely 1–3 mi depending on where the truck-accessible road ends** — needs an on-the-ground or satellite check.

**Water:** Rock Creek — a stocked/wild trout stream, so treat water and expect anglers near the hatchery end.

**Gold evidence:** Same 1911 Gennett-tract citation as Candidate A explicitly names "**Rock Creek** and Noontootly Creek area of Fannin, Gilmer, Lumpkin and Union counties" as the historic tract. [GON – Story of Blue Ridge WMA](https://gon.com/hunting/the-story-of-blue-ridge-the-first-wma)

**Land status:** Chattahoochee NF, non-Wilderness, part of Blue Ridge WMA (20,900 ac, Fannin/Union/Lumpkin, USFS-managed recreation). [GA DNR – Blue Ridge WMA](https://georgiawildlife.com/blue-ridge-wma) A WMA hunting/fishing license or GA Lands Pass may be required for all persons on-site even if not hunting — **confirm this applies to Rock Creek specifically**, since some sources describe it as a general NF recreation site rather than WMA proper. [source used for adjacent Wildcat Creek WMA rule](https://www.n-georgia.com/wildcat-creek-campground-upper.html)

**Hazards:** Firearms deer season (WMA land may have different/earlier hunt days than general NF — check GA DNR WMA hunt calendar); proximity to a working fish hatchery (respect posted hatchery boundaries).

**Crowding:** Popular trout-fishing dispersed camping corridor — moderate pressure near the road; better once away from vehicle sites.

**Drive time from Vogel:** Estimated 35–45 min via GA 180/US19-129 to Suches, then GA 60 N — **not routed.**

## Candidate C: Duncan Ridge Trail / Benton MacKaye Trail at the Toccoa River suspension bridge

**Trailhead:** Access varies by segment; the swinging-bridge crossing of the Toccoa River is roughly mid-trail on the 35.5-mi Duncan Ridge Trail. [Wikipedia – Duncan Ridge Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Ridge_Trail) One published segment starts at Bryson Gap and descends 2.7 mi to the river (elevation 2,770 ft → 1,920 ft). [N2Backpacking – BMT/DRT Bryson Gap to Toccoa River](https://n2backpacking.com/hikes/georgia/benton-mackaye-trail-bryson-gap-to-toccoa-river/) **Exact trailhead coordinates for Bryson Gap were not found in this pass — UNVERIFIED, needs a CalTopo/Gaia check before use.**

**Route:** Bryson Gap → Toccoa River, 2.7 mi one-way, losing ~850 ft (all downhill in, uphill out) — fits the group's pace easily as an out-and-back. A 260-ft suspension footbridge crosses the river at the campsite cluster. [N2Backpacking](https://n2backpacking.com/hikes/georgia/benton-mackaye-trail-bryson-gap-to-toccoa-river/)

**Campsite(s):** Established campsites on both sides of the Toccoa River suspension bridge; a secondary option with a spring near Bryson Gap itself if the group prefers to camp high and day-hike down to pan. [N2Backpacking](https://n2backpacking.com/hikes/georgia/benton-mackaye-trail-bryson-gap-to-toccoa-river/) No specific lat/lon published for either site in sources found — **UNVERIFIED coordinates.**

**Water:** Toccoa River — the largest stream in this batch of candidates, good swimming/panning gravel bars typical of a mid-size mountain river.

**Gold evidence:** Weaker than Candidates A/B — no source found naming a specific historic mine or placer directly on the Toccoa River itself in this drainage; the inference rests on the same broad Fannin/Union county goldfield context (Georgia Gold Belt) and the adjacent Gennett "Rock Creek and Noontootly Creek" tract a few miles south. **Treat gold evidence here as regional-context only, not creek-specific — weakest of the top four candidates on this criterion.**

**Land status:** Chattahoochee NF, non-Wilderness (Duncan Ridge NRA/Scenic corridor status, not designated Wilderness). [Wikipedia – Duncan Ridge Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Ridge_Trail)

**Hazards:** River crossing via suspension bridge (can sway; supervise newer hikers); larger/deeper water than the creek candidates — watch current if panning from the bank into the river itself vs. a side seep.

**Crowding:** Toccoa River suspension bridge is a known, moderately popular DRT/BMT waypoint — expect some thru-hiker or day-hiker traffic, less than Three Forks.

**Drive time from Vogel:** Estimated 45–55 min, north-central Fannin Co. — **not routed.**

## Candidate D: Amicalola Creek, Dawson Forest WMA (Amicalola tract), upper NF reaches outside Amicalola Falls SP

**Trailhead/access:** Dawson Forest WMA — 25,500 acres in five tracts (Wildcat Creek, Goethe, Burnt Mountain, Amicalola, City of Atlanta), USFS/GA DNR managed, with marked improved trails and old roadbed track open to public use. [GA DNR – Dawson Forest WMA](https://gadnr.org/dawson-forest-wma) A river-access point with parking exists on GA 53 at the Amicalola River bridge (34.**UNVERIFIED exact coordinate**, description only: "north side of the bridge on Rt 53"), mainly used for tubing/kayaking/fishing — could serve as a put-in for an upstream hike/pan approach on NF land above the state park boundary. [Mountain Stewards – Amicalola River Trails Access](https://mountainstewards.org/amicalola-river-trails/)

**Route:** UNVERIFIED — no specific hike-in trail to a documented placer reach was found; would need on-the-ground WMA-map reconnaissance (GA DNR publishes a Dawson Forest WMA map) to route a ~5 mi hike-in avoiding Amicalola Falls State Park land (panning banned in the park).

**Gold evidence:** Strongest documented gold history of any candidate in this file. Historic mines named directly for the creek/area: **Missing Link Mine** (aka Ellsworth Mine, Magic Mine, **Baby Placer**, **Amicalola Mine**, Miller Placer) and the **Nina Head Placer**, both in Dawson County. [The Diggings summary via search](https://thediggings.com/usa/georgia/dawson-ga085) Dawson County was mined heavily during the 1829+ Georgia Gold Rush. [Dawson County govt history page](https://www.dawsoncountyga.gov/448/History)

**Land status:** Mixed — Amicalola Falls State Park (panning banned) sits at the creek's dramatic upper falls; Dawson Forest WMA covers much of the surrounding NF/WMA land where standard hand-panning rules would apply. A WMA license/GA Lands Pass is required on-site. **The exact boundary between park land and open WMA/NF land along the creek needs map confirmation before committing** — this is the key open question for this candidate.

**Hazards:** WMA hunting pressure (deer season opens Oct 17 — this is prime WMA hunting land, likely busier with hunters than the more remote candidates); need to positively confirm you are outside the state park boundary before panning.

**Crowding:** Popular WMA for mountain biking, hiking, tubing near the falls/river-access parking — moderate pressure near road access, likely much lower once away from GA 53.

**Drive time from Vogel:** Estimated 70–80 min — furthest-south candidate in this file, well south toward Dawsonville. **Likely marginal against a 75-min truck-access budget — flag as a stretch option.**

**Verdict:** Best gold-history citation of the batch, but weakest on route/campsite specificity and drive time. Worth a follow-up call to Dawson Forest WMA office rather than a top pick as-is.

## Candidate E: Wildcat Creek headwaters, Rabun County (Wildcat Rd, above Lake Burton)

**Trailhead/access:** Wildcat Creek Campground #2 (Upper), reached via Wildcat Rd, gravel, roughly 4 mi up-valley from Wildcat #1. [The Dyrt – Wildcat Creek Campground #2 Upper](https://thedyrt.com/camping/georgia/wildcat-2) Primitive dispersed sites, no amenities, "buried deep in the North Georgia mountains along the headwaters of Wildcat Creek." [n-georgia.com](https://www.n-georgia.com/wildcat-creek-campground-upper.html) A hiking trail (Wildcat Creek/Sliding Rock Trail) continues past the upper campground. [search summary, AllTrails: Wildcat Creek and Turner Trail]

**Route:** From the upper dispersed-camping cluster, hike further upstream along the Wildcat Creek trail corridor for a quieter overnight site — **exact one-way mileage and gain UNVERIFIED**, would need AllTrails/Gaia GPS trail data pulled directly.

**Gold evidence:** The Rabun County Historical Society documents gold mining specifically on **Wildcat Creek**, on land owned by James Stonecypher on the "western shore of Lake Burton" (pre-dating the lake, which was later impounded), as part of a combined $150,000–$250,000 output from all Dick's/Moccasin/Wildcat Creek operations in the county. [Rabun County Historical Society – Gold Fever in Rabun County](https://rabunhistory.org/articles/gold-fever-in-rabun-county-and-northeast-georgia-muddy-creeks-18-pennyweights-and-meager-or-no-profits/) Note the historic workings were nearer the creek's mouth (now under/near Lake Burton); the upper Wildcat Rd dispersed-camping reaches are the same named creek but farther upstream from the documented historic site — same waterway, unconfirmed whether gold-bearing gravel extends that far up.

**Land status:** Chattahoochee NF, Tallulah Ranger District — non-Wilderness (well east of Southern Nantahala/Rich Mountain Wilderness boundaries). Access requires a hunting/fishing license or GA Lands Pass for all persons per WMA-style signage noted at nearby Wildcat sites — **verify whether this specific reach is WMA or plain NF.**

**Hazards:** Firearms season; creek crossings; narrow gravel road (Wildcat Rd) — check clearance needs, likely fine for trucks per camper reports.

**Crowding:** Described as a rugged, primitive, less-trafficked dispersed area relative to the drive-up Wildcat #1 campground — reasonable solitude prospects.

**Drive time from Vogel:** Estimated 65–75 min via US76 E toward Clayton/Lake Burton area — **not routed**, near the outer edge of the truck-access budget.

## Candidate F: Moccasin Creek, upper NF reaches above Moccasin Creek State Park (Lake Burton, Rabun Co.)

**Access:** Moccasin Creek itself flows into Lake Burton through Moccasin Creek State Park (panning banned — state park). [Wikipedia – Moccasin Creek State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moccasin_Creek_State_Park) Upstream of the park boundary, the creek runs through Chattahoochee NF land before the park — a hike-in from an upstream NF trailhead/FS road could reach legal panning water while avoiding the park. **No specific upper-Moccasin-Creek NF trailhead or FS road number was identified in this pass — needs a follow-up search** (likely accessed off GA 197 or a spur FS road north of the park).

**Gold evidence:** Documented historic gold mining on Moccasin Creek on James Stonecypher's land, western shore of Lake Burton (same source and same combined county total as Wildcat Creek, above). [Rabun County Historical Society](https://rabunhistory.org/articles/gold-fever-in-rabun-county-and-northeast-georgia-muddy-creeks-18-pennyweights-and-meager-or-no-profits/)

**Land status:** Mixed — the lower creek and its Lake Burton confluence are inside the state park (banned); upper NF reaches are plausible but unconfirmed for legal panning access.

**Verdict:** Real, well-documented gold history, but this candidate needs real legwork (exact trailhead, upstream FS road, confirmed park boundary) before it's trip-ready. Listed for completeness, **not recommended without a follow-up research pass or a ranger-district phone call.**

**Drive time from Vogel:** Estimated 60–70 min via GA 197 — **not routed.**

## Ranking (this file's candidates only)

1. **Candidate A — Three Forks / Noontootla Creek (BMT/AT).** Best combination of a real, creek-specific historic gold citation (Gennett "Rock Creek and Noontootly Creek" tract + thediggings Fannin placer/Hackney entry), a documented easy-grade hike-in well under the group's 5 mi/day pace, established camping, non-Wilderness status, and a reasonable estimated drive time.
2. **Candidate B — Rock Creek dispersed (Fannin).** Same strong gold citation as A (same historic tract, same source), good truck access, but weaker on the "hike in, not drive to" requirement — needs the road-end-to-hike-in gap confirmed.
3. **Candidate D — Amicalola Creek / Dawson Forest WMA.** Strongest individual mine citations of any candidate (named Amicalola Mine, Missing Link, Nina Head Placer) but the weakest on route specificity, likely over/near the 75-min drive budget, and carries a real state-park-boundary legal risk that needs to be resolved before use.
4. **Candidate C — Duncan Ridge/Toccoa River suspension bridge.** Great camping/trail logistics (established sites, moderate mileage, iconic bridge) but the gold evidence is regional-context only, not creek-specific — the weakest gold citation of the top four.
5. **Candidate E — Wildcat Creek headwaters (Rabun).** Real, sourced gold history, but the historic workings are downstream near Lake Burton while the drive-to camping is upstream on the same creek — unconfirmed whether the gold-bearing reach and the camping reach overlap. Also near the outer edge of drive-time budget.
6. **Candidate F — Moccasin Creek upper reaches.** Same quality gold citation as E, but this candidate is the least trip-ready — no confirmed upstream trailhead/FS road was found; needs a dedicated follow-up pass.
