## Backpack (40–60L)
- **Budget — Osprey Rook 50, $155, 66 oz.** Real Osprey suspension/warranty at the lowest price serious reviewers still endorse. Heavy but forgiving for a first trip.
- **Value — REI Flash 55, $229, 45 oz.** The community's price/weight/comfort sweet spot under 30 lb loads; regularly the first "real" UL pack people buy.
- **Premium — Gossamer Gear Mariposa 60, $315, 34.2 oz.** PCT/CDT staple, comfortably carries 25–30 lb at true UL weight. (ULA Circuit, $300/38.5 oz, is the direct competitor — pick by torso fit.)

## Tent / Shelter (2-person, split weight)
- **Budget — REI Trail Hut 2, $159, 91 oz (45.5 oz/person).** Freestanding double-wall, handles rain well, easiest for a first tent.
- **Value — Durston X-Mid 2, $319, 31.3 oz (15.7 oz/person).** The most-recommended value UL shelter anywhere — best space-to-weight in category. Needs 2 trekking poles, order 2–4 weeks ahead.
- **Premium — Zpacks Duplex, $699, 18.5 oz (9.25 oz/person).** The benchmark UL 2P shelter, but single-wall DCF condenses hardest in exactly this trip's humid low-30s conditions — flagged as the riskiest pick, not recommended as a first purchase for this trip.

## Sleeping bag vs. quilt (~20°F)
Beginner note included in JSON: a quilt has no zipper/hood — it drapes over you and straps under the pad, skipping insulation on the pad-compressed underside to save weight. Needs a warm hat and careful footbox/edge sealing; a bag is more forgiving for a first-timer.
- **Budget — Marmot Trestles 15 (or Kelty Cosmic 20 Synthetic), $99, 53 oz.** Synthetic fill shrugs off damp GA fall air; full hood/zipper is the most beginner-proof option.
- **Value — Enlightened Equipment Revelation 20 (stock, 850fp), $355, 26 oz.** Roughly half the bulk/weight of the budget bag; the most-recommended value quilt on r/Ultralight.
- **Premium — Katabatic Gear Alsek 22, $399, 25.5 oz.** Endgame 3-season quilt; users report comfort 6–7°F below rating thanks to its pad-attachment system — real margin for a cold night.

## Sleeping pad (R≥4 required for October)
- **Budget — Klymit Insulated Static V, $74.95, 25 oz, R 4.4.** Actually clears the R4 bar (unlike most sub-$50 pads) — confirm "Insulated" in the listing, not the Lite/V2.
- **Value — NEMO Tensor All-Season Insulated, $199.95, 14.1 oz, R 5.4.** Class-leading warmth-to-weight; the standard (non-All-Season) Tensor is only R3.5 and would NOT clear the bar — don't substitute it.
- **Premium — NEMO Tensor Extreme Conditions, $259.95, 19 oz, R 8.5.** Serious overkill for GA in October; the endgame pick only if backpacking becomes a habit beyond this trip.

## Pillow
- **Budget — DIY stuff sack + spare clothes, $0.** The most common community answer — free, uses gear already carried.
- **Value — Sea to Summit Aeros Down, $54.95, 2.5 oz.** Quiet TPU bladder, down insulation adds real warmth at the head.
- **Premium — NEMO Fillo Elite, $54.95, 2.5 oz.** Essentially a coin-flip with the S2S pick — foam/air hybrid feel preference, not a capability gap.

## Groundsheet
- **Budget — DIY polycryo (window shrink film), ~$8.** Lightest, cheapest, community-standard "don't overthink it" pick; more fragile.
- **Value — DIY Tyvek HomeWrap, ~$15/roll.** Heavier but far more puncture/abrasion resistant for rocky GA sites; a roll covers the whole group for years.
- **Premium — Durston/Zpacks branded footprint, ~$39.** Convenience purchase only (exact fit), no functional advantage over DIY — skip unless buying the matching tent.

## Stuff / dry sacks
- **Budget — Ziploc freezer bags + cheap stuff sack, ~$6.** Fine for a single overnight since the pack liner does the real waterproofing.
- **Value — Sea to Summit Ultra-Sil Dry Sack (2-bag set), ~$40.** Genuinely waterproof roll-top; the default "just buy these" community answer.
- **Premium — Hyperlite Mountain Gear DCF stuff sacks, ~$33 ea.** Fully waterproof fabric, no coating to delaminate — overkill for this one trip, worth it only for ongoing use.

## Pack liner
- **Budget — Heavy-duty compactor/contractor bag, ~$3.** The single highest-value item on the whole list for this trip's rain forecast; noisy but free and fully waterproof. Already the existing gear.md's pick — confirmed correct against community sourcing.
- **Value — Litesmith Nylofume liner, ~$6, 0.9 oz.** Lighter/more puncture-resistant than a trash bag, but noisier; marginal upgrade, not essential.
- **Premium — Sea to Summit eVent Dry Pack Liner, $44.95, 3.5 oz.** Weakest "premium" case in this document — most experienced UL hikers explicitly stick with the free trash-bag option even long-term. Listed for completeness, not recommended.

## Key takeaway for the Captain
The trash-compactor-bag pack liner and DIY polycryo/Tyvek groundsheet are not "cheaping out" — they are the community's actual first-choice picks, not just the budget fallback. The one place NOT to cut corners is the sleeping pad's R-value: several popular pads (Tensor standard R3.5, Ether Light XT R3.2) look similar but do not clear the R≥4 bar needed for low-30s nights at 3,000–4,000 ft — the Klymit Insulated Static V or NEMO Tensor All-Season are the two verified-R4+ picks.
