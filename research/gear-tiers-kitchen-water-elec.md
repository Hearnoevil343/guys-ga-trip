# Gear Tiers — Kitchen, Water, Electronics, Misc (GA Gold Trip, Oct 2026)

Companion to `gear-tiers-kitchen-water-elec.json`. Prices/weights as of 2026-09-21 where sourced this session; "EST" in the JSON `weight_note`/notes fields means informed by category norms rather than a single verified spec page — confirm before buying. Full citations are in each JSON entry's `verdict_sources`.

## Reasoning per category

**Stove (group ÷6):** BRS-3000T remains the community's disposable-budget stove despite QC complaints. Soto WindMaster is the widely-cited "upgrade once and stop thinking about it" pick, and matters more here than usual because Oct mornings in the low 30s°F reward the WindMaster's better cold/wind performance. Jetboil Stash topped the 2025 PCT survey and is the integrated-system premium option if the group wants to skip a separate pot.

**Cook pot (group ÷6):** For one overnight, a cheap aluminum pot works fine — titanium buys weight savings the group doesn't need to pay for on a single trip. TOAKS 750ml is the default community recommendation if going titanium; Evernew's insulated-handle pasta pot is the enthusiast pick but flagged as increasingly hard to find in the US.

**Fuel (group ÷6):** Not really a tiered category — the community consensus is fuel brand barely matters above freezing; blend ratio (butane/propane mix) only matters below ~20°F, which this trip won't see. Buy the smallest canister that covers 6 people boiling water twice.

**Eating utensil/cup (personal):** Functionally near-identical across all three tiers; recommend the Captain skip the premium tier here and put the money toward the water filter or bag instead.

**Water filter (group ÷6):** Sawyer Squeeze is still the most-used filter on both PCT/AT surveys, but its freeze-crack risk is a real concern for this trip's low-30s night — Sawyer's own FAQ says to sleep with it in the bag. Katadyn BeFree trades some durability for much faster flow. Grayl GeoPress is heavier but purifies (not just filters) and lets one person fill for the whole group fast without squeezing — arguably the best group-logistics fit despite the weight penalty.

**Water bottles/bladders (personal):** The Smartwater-bottle hack is genuinely endorsed by the UL community, not just a cheap compromise — recommend it as default. CNOC Vecto is worth it only if paired with the Sawyer/BeFree filter tiers for gravity/scoop filling from a shallow creek.

**Food storage (group ÷6, Southeast-specific):** This is the category where "what serious hikers actually use in the Southeast" diverges most from a generic UL gear list. DIY hangs are cheap but increasingly distrusted in bear-dense GA due to food-conditioned bears; Ursack Major (+ OPSak) is what SectionHiker/CleverHiker/Trek forums consistently point to as the Southeast/AT-region default; BearVault is the premium hard-canister pick but is overkill for GA where canisters aren't mandated — recommend Ursack as the trip's actual pick.

**Headlamp (personal):** Black Diamond Spot 400 is CleverHiker's current Best Overall and the clear value pick. Nitecore NU25's rechargeable design is the lightest option but creates a dependency on the power bank as its only mid-trip recharge method — a real tradeoff on a no-signal, no-outlet trip.

**Power bank (personal):** Nitecore's NB10000 line is the consistent weight-to-capacity leader across 2026 gear roundups. Gen 4 (Feb 2026 release) is only a marginal improvement over Gen 3 — recommend Gen 3 (value tier) as the better buy.

**Satellite messenger (group ÷6):** Renting the inReach Mini 2 for the trip (as in the existing plan) remains the most cost-effective choice for occasional use — buying only pencils out if the group expects to keep doing backcountry trips. ZOLEO undercuts Garmin on both device price and monthly plan and is easier for non-hiker family contacts to text, at the cost of no standalone screen.

**Phone navigation app (personal):** CalTopo (free, printed) is confirmed as the right default — a printed map has zero battery/signal dependency, which matters most on a no-cell-signal backcountry night. FarOut's premium value (crowd-sourced water/campsite intel) only applies if the specific GA route/trail actually has FarOut coverage — verify before buying.

**Knife/multitool (personal):** Opinel No.8 is the lightest, cheapest capable option. Leatherman Wingman is the value multitool (real pliers/scissors for camp tasks); Skeletool CX is the lighter multitool serious hikers upgrade to when they want fewer, better tools instead of a bulky full kit.

**First aid kit (group ÷6):** Matches the existing plan's split — AMK Ultralight/Watertight .7 (value) carried on the overnight, AMK Mountain Series Explorer (premium/group-sized) stays at base camp. DIY is cheaper and more tailored but the community flags it as higher-risk without someone who actually knows first aid assembling it.

**Toiletries/trowel (personal):** Trowel material barely matters for one trip in soft GA forest soil — a $4-10 plastic trowel is genuinely "good enough." The Deuce of Spades titanium trowel is a near-meme upgrade in r/Ultralight but not necessary here.

**Trekking poles (personal):** Cascade Mountain Tech carbon poles are the standout "bops for the price" pick — near-premium weight at budget-aluminum pricing, with the known caveat that quick-lock levers can slip under heavy load (check tension before descents). Black Diamond Distance Carbon Z is the folding premium pick, useful mainly if poles need to be quickly stowed for creek panning.

**Sit pad (personal):** A free cut scrap of CCF foam is functionally identical to a bought pad. Therm-a-Rest Z Seat is the classic, rarely-upgraded-from community standard. Gossamer Gear's Thinlight pad is only a meaningful upgrade if paired with a frameless pack it can also stiffen.

**Camp shoes (personal):** Crocs Classic Clog is the near-universal trail-camp sight and clear value pick. Xero Shoes Z-Trail is the minimalist/thru-hiker upgrade with better wet-rock traction, at the cost of cushioning. Either way, keep camp shoes separate from the dedicated neoprene-sock creek-panning footwear already planned in the existing `gear.md` Kit 3.

## Key flags for the Captain

- **Water filter freezing risk is real for this trip** — whichever filter is chosen, it must be kept warm (jacket pocket by day, sleeping bag at night) on the low-30s°F night, or dried out completely (Grayl avoids this issue entirely since it has no hollow-fiber membrane to freeze).
- **Ursack, not a hang, matches what Southeast/GA backpackers actually use** — worth updating the existing plan's "food storage" line from a generic hang/vehicle-storage note to specifically recommend Ursack Major + OPSak for the backcountry night.
- **inReach Mini 2 price has risen to $349.99 at REI** as of this search (previously ~$300 in the existing gear.md) — rental remains the better value for a single trip either way.
- **FarOut coverage should be verified for the actual GA route** before paying for it — it's the premium nav pick only if the specific trail has active crowd-sourced data.
